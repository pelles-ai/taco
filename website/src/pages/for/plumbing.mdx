## Where TACO fits your scope

Plumbing lives primarily in **CSI division 22 (Plumbing)** with frequent crossings into **33 (Site Utilities)** for water service and storm drainage, and **23 (HVAC)** for hydronic piping shared with mechanical scope. The TACO `bom-v1` and `quote-v1` schemas were designed with this kind of multi-division-but-trade-coherent scope in mind.

What's *different* about plumbing vs. mechanical:

- **The supplier ecosystem is fragmented.** Plumbing wholesalers (Ferguson, F.W. Webb, Hajoca, Winsupply, regional independents) compete on inventory and lead time more than on price. A typed quote that includes per-item lead time is more valuable than one that doesn't.
- **Medical gas is its own world.** Spec sections 22 60–66 (medical gas) require certified installers and dedicated suppliers; treat it as a separate skill or sub-agent rather than as a normal plumbing scope item.
- **Water-authority approvals add lifecycle steps.** Tap permits, backflow assemblies, water meter sizing — these are coordination tasks the orchestrator should know about, not pure scope items.
- **Service vs. trim split.** Underground / rough-in scope is sequenced very differently from finish trim, and they often go to different procurement cycles.

## Schema fit for plumbing

`bom-v1` line items applied to plumbing scope:

| Field | Plumbing example |
|------|---------------------|
| `description` | `"Copper Type L domestic cold water, hard-drawn"` |
| `quantity` | `1280` |
| `unit` | `LF` for piping; `EA` for fixtures + valves + meters; `LS` for installation packages |
| `size` | `"1 inch"` for piping, `"4 inch"` for service, fixture model numbers for trim |
| `material` | `"copper"`, `"PEX"`, `"CPVC"`, `"cast-iron"`, `"PVC"`, `"PP"`, `"stainless"` |
| `specSection` | `"22 11 16"` for domestic water distribution, `"22 13 16"` for sanitary, `"22 60 00"` for medical gas |
| `sourceSheet` | `"P-201"` (water service), `"P-301"` (sanitary), `"PM-101"` (medical gas) |
| `location` | `"Level 2 Wing B"`, `"Building service entrance"`, `"OR-1 Med Gas wall"` |
| `alternates[]` | acceptable substitutions (PEX in place of copper, PVC in place of cast-iron for sanitary above-grade) |

A field worth highlighting for plumbing: **`metadata.flaggedItems[]`**. Plumbing takeoffs reliably produce items needing human review — unusual fitting sizes, ambiguous fixture model numbers, missing valve specifications. Surface them in `flaggedItems` so the estimator (or human PM) catches them before quotes go out.

The wholesaler hop is the high-value one. Fan out across three to five wholesalers using the [BOM-to-Quote Marketplace pattern](/docs/cookbook/bom-to-quote-marketplace), then level on the policy your project actually cares about — usually a combination of total price, lead time relative to rough-in start, and preferred-supplier rebate programs.

## Platforms typically integrated

- **Estimating** — Trimble FastPIPE (purpose-built for plumbing), Trimble Accubid, McCormick, ConWize, PlanSwift with plumbing tags
- **Design** — Revit MEP, AutoCAD MEP, Plumber's CAD (Trimble), CADpipe
- **Wholesaler catalogs** — Ferguson, F.W. Webb, Hajoca, Winsupply (each has its own API; sidecar pattern wraps them)
- **Coordination** — Navisworks, Revizto
- **Project management** — Procore, ACC, Sage 300 (common in mid-sized plumbing shops)

Declare your relevant `integrations[]` on the agent card so GC orchestrators that already speak the same wholesalers find you naturally.

## Common plumbing-specific pitfalls

**Mixing copper grades silently.** Type L, Type M, and Type K copper have different costs and code allowances. A takeoff that lumps them as "copper" loses material information the estimator needs. Use `material: "copper-L"` (or whatever your convention is) — pick one and document it.

**Medical gas in a normal plumbing skill.** Medical gas requires NFPA 99 certified installers, dedicated brazing, and specialty supplier networks (Praxair, Linde, Air Products). Don't expose medical gas as a normal plumbing skill — declare a separate `medical-gas-installation` skill or sub-agent with appropriate `csi_divisions: ["22"]` and a note in the description. Mixing the two in one estimator produces underbid pricing.

**Service entrance forgotten.** The water service from the main to the building is often a separate scope (under CSI 33 site utilities) and often a separate cost basis (water authority fees, tap permits, meter sizing). If your takeoff agent silently includes or excludes the service entrance, downstream estimates will be off by a large amount. Be explicit in `metadata.notes`.

**Backflow assemblies treated as line items.** A backflow preventer is a line item with installation costs, but it's also a code-required device with testing certification and ongoing inspection schedule. Track it in the BOM, but flag it in `flaggedItems` so commissioning and operations are aware.

**Storm vs sanitary confusion.** Storm and sanitary look similar in BIM but have very different code requirements and pipe materials. If your takeoff agent is reading from a model with poor system tagging, it will silently mix them. Validate against the spec section (`22 13` for sanitary, `22 14` for storm).

## How to start

1. **Build a stub.** Follow [Build a Custom Agent](/docs/getting-started/build-agent). Declare `trade: "plumbing"`, `csi_divisions: ["22"]` (add `"33"` if you do site utilities, `"23"` for hydronic crossover).
2. **Pick one skill first.** Most plumbing shops start with `estimate`. Add `material-procurement` later when you're ready to wrap your wholesaler integrations.
3. **Add code-compliance audit as a differentiator.** A `spec-compliance-check` skill that validates against IPC/UPC tables makes you genuinely valuable to a GC orchestrator beyond pricing.
4. **Run the conformance runner.** [`/conformance`](/conformance) catches structural issues before any GC's orchestrator filters you out.
