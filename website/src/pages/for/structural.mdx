## Where TACO fits your scope

Structural work spans **CSI division 03 (Concrete)**, **04 (Masonry)**, and **05 (Metals — structural steel, miscellaneous metals, and metal fabrications)**. Many shops specialize in one division; some integrators span all three. The TACO Agent Card supports either pattern via the `csi_divisions[]` field.

What's *different* about structural vs. other trades:

- **The sequence is the project.** Structural rough-in for floor N has to complete before floor N+1 can start. A delay in your shop's scope cascades into every trade above. The schedule-aware reconciliation pattern matters more here than anywhere else.
- **Shop drawings are first-class artifacts.** Fabricators (steel detailers, rebar shop drawings, precast shops) produce their own typed deliverables. The `submittal-review` task type maps directly.
- **Embed coordination crosses trades.** Anchor bolts, electrical conduits embedded in slabs, plumbing sleeves, mechanical embeds — every embed is a typed coordination point. RFIs and clash detection drive most of this.
- **Concrete placement scheduling has tight windows.** Pour day is a critical-path event with weather dependencies, batch plant scheduling, and inspection coordination. The schedule-coordination task type, integrated with weather and ready-mix availability, is real.

## Schema fit for structural

`bom-v1` line items applied to structural scope:

| Field | Structural example |
|------|---------------------|
| `description` | `"W14x90 wide flange beam, A992 steel, shop-fab"`, `"Grade 60 rebar #5"`, `"3000 psi concrete, normal-weight"` |
| `quantity` | `48` (pieces), `12400` (lbs of rebar), `185` (CY concrete) |
| `unit` | `EA` for steel members; `LB` or `TON` for rebar; `CY` for concrete; `SF` for formwork; `LF` for misc metal |
| `size` | `"W14x90"`, `"#5"`, `"3000 psi"` |
| `material` | `"A992-steel"`, `"grade-60-rebar"`, `"epoxy-coated-rebar"`, `"3000-psi-normal-weight-concrete"` |
| `specSection` | `"05 12 13"` (structural steel framing), `"03 21 00"` (reinforcing steel), `"03 30 00"` (cast-in-place concrete) |
| `sourceSheet` | `"S-201"` (framing plans), `"S-301"` (sections), `"S-401"` (details) |
| `location` | `"Level 2 framing"`, `"Foundation, west bay"` |
| `alternates[]` | acceptable substitutions (e.g. W14x99 substitution for W14x90 if specified light section unavailable) |

A field worth highlighting for structural: **`specSection`**. Structural specs reference industry standards heavily (AISC, ACI, ASTM); the spec section is how downstream consumers know which standard applies. Don't truncate it.

The shop drawing review loop (detailer → EOR → detailer) is the bottleneck on most steel jobs. Typed `rfi-v1` for each review comment, with `referenceTaskIds` linking back to the original submittal, makes the round-trip auditable in ways PDF markup never was.

## Platforms typically integrated

- **Steel detailing** — Tekla Structures, SDS/2, Bentley ProStructures, Advance Steel
- **Rebar detailing** — Tekla, aSa, Rebar2000
- **Structural design** — RAM Structural System, ETABS, RISA, ProStructures, Robot
- **Concrete batching** — CommandAlkon, JWS, Conexpo's own catalogs (per regional batch plant)
- **Project management** — Procore (with structural-specific modules), ACC, Tekla PowerFab for fabrication
- **BIM coordination** — Navisworks, Revizto, Tekla BIMsight

Steel and rebar shops in particular often have mature internal tooling (Tekla PowerFab, aSa) that pre-dates any cloud platform. The sidecar pattern wraps these without rewrites.

## Common structural-specific pitfalls

**Treating a shop drawing rev as a new project.** Shop drawings cycle through multiple revisions (Rev A pending review → Rev B with comments → Rev C resubmittal → IFC for issue-for-construction). Each rev is a typed `rfi-v1` (or `submittal-review`) round-trip, linked via `referenceTaskIds`. Treating each rev as a new project loses the audit trail.

**Misclassifying rebar substitutions.** Substituting Grade 80 rebar for Grade 60 reduces required area by ~25% per ACI 318, but the GC's pricing model might not know. Don't silently allow substitutions — flag them in `flaggedItems` so the EOR's responder agent confirms the design intent.

**Forgetting embed coordination in the BOM.** Anchor bolts, embed plates, and curb angles are all structural-side line items but are physically installed by adjacent trades (electrical, mechanical embeds especially). If your structural BOM omits embeds because "the GC handles those," coordination breaks. Include them with `location` and `metadata.notes` indicating responsibility.

**Ignoring pour-day weather risk.** A concrete pour scheduled in freezing weather requires admixtures, heated batching, and post-pour curing protection — all of which are line items, not weather contingencies. Your schedule-coordination agent should query weather and flag affected pours.

**Steel takeoff without erection sequence.** A BOM of 1,200 steel members is a procurement document; without erection sequence (which member arrives in what order), it's an erection planning gap. Either include sequence in `metadata` or emit a parallel `schedule-v1` artifact tied to the BOM.

## How to start

1. **Build a stub.** Follow [Build a Custom Agent](/docs/getting-started/build-agent). Declare your division — typically `csi_divisions: ["03"]` (concrete), `["05"]` (steel), or `["03", "05"]` if you span both.
2. **Pick the highest-value skill first.** For most structural shops, that's either `estimate` (you bid frequently) or `submittal-review` (you handle a lot of shop drawing reviews on your projects). Add the other later.
3. **Wire to your existing detailing platform.** Tekla, SDS/2, RAM — the handler is a translator between the platform's exports and TACO's typed artifacts.
4. **Run the conformance runner.** [`/conformance`](/conformance) catches structural issues — pun intended — before downstream consumers see them.
