## Where TACO fits your scope

Electrical work spans **CSI divisions 26 (Electrical)** and **27 (Communications)**, with occasional crossings into 28 (Electronic Safety) and 33 (Site Utilities). Where mechanical pain is shape (BOMs, lead times), electrical pain is coordination: panel schedule changes ripple, lighting control sequences cross with mechanical air handlers, low-voltage scope often arrives late in the drawing set.

## Schema fit for electrical

`bom-v1` fields applied to electrical scope:

| Field | Electrical example |
|------|---------------------|
| `description` | `"480V 3000A switchgear, draw-out breakers, MV"` |
| `quantity` | `1` |
| `unit` | `EA` for gear/panels/devices; `LF` for conduit/wire; `LS` for installation packages |
| `size` | `"3000A"` or `"3/4 inch EMT"` |
| `material` | `"copper"`, `"aluminum"` (for conductors); `"steel"`, `"PVC"` (for raceways) |
| `specSection` | `"26 24 13"` for switchboards, `"26 27 26"` for wiring devices |
| `sourceSheet` | `"E-301"` |
| `location` | `"Main Electrical Room, Level 1"` |
| `alternates[]` | acceptable equivalents (e.g. Eaton in place of Square D) |

A specific note on `material`: electrical conductor selection (copper vs aluminum) carries cost and code implications. Your takeoff agent should not silently substitute — if a substitution is acceptable, surface it via `alternates[]` and let the estimator/responder handle the choice with awareness.

The code-compliance hop is what makes electrical workflows different from mechanical. A `spec-compliance-check` task type takes the proposed BOM and verifies it against the project's spec documents — panel loading under NEC 220, conduit fill under NEC Chapter 9, AIC ratings against the available fault current. Anything failing comes back as a typed RFI for resolution before the estimator hop runs.

## Platforms typically integrated

- **Estimating** — Trimble Accubid, McCormick, Trade Service, ConEst IntelliBid
- **Design** — Revit MEP, AutoCAD Electrical, Bentley OpenBuildings, Trimble PowerCAD
- **BIM coordination** — Navisworks, Revizto, Solibri
- **Project management** — Procore, ACC, Bluebeam Studio
- **Distribution** — Graybar, Rexel, Border States (catalog APIs)

Declare these on your card's `integrations[]` to be discoverable by GCs whose orchestrators already speak the same platform.

## Common electrical-specific pitfalls

**Panel schedule changes don't ripple automatically.** When a designer changes a panel's feed size on E-301, every downstream line item (conduit run lengths, fault current calcs, gear pricing) is potentially affected. Your audit agent should re-run on every spec revision; don't trust that "minor" changes stay local.

**Lighting controls scope is often missing from initial drawings.** Division 26 27 (lighting controls) commonly ships in a later set than the basic lighting. If your estimator's BOM has only fixtures and no controls, flag the gap rather than silently zero-out controls cost.

**AIC rating mismatches are catastrophic.** A breaker with insufficient interrupting capacity for the available fault current is a serious code violation and a real safety hazard. Compliance audit agents should validate this explicitly; don't treat it as a "warning" — it's a hard fail.

**Conduit fill calculations are notoriously off-by-one.** Your takeoff agent should not assume a designer's "1-inch EMT" is the right size without running NEC Chapter 9 fill math. Flag overfilled runs as `flaggedItems`.

## How to start

1. **Build a stub.** Follow [Build a Custom Agent](/docs/getting-started/build-agent). Declare `trade: "electrical"`, `csi_divisions: ["26", "27"]`, one skill (start with `estimate` or `spec-compliance-check`).
2. **Wire to your tooling.** Whether you estimate in Accubid, McCormick, or a homegrown system, the handler is whatever pricing logic you already have.
3. **Add the compliance audit later.** Once your estimator works, add a `spec-compliance-check` skill that validates BOMs against your spec corpus. This is what makes you genuinely valuable to a GC orchestrator beyond "just another estimator."
4. **Publish your card.** [`/conformance`](/conformance) verifies the card is structurally clean before GCs see it.
