## Where TACO fits your scope

Mechanical work cuts across CSI divisions **21 (Fire Suppression)**, **22 (Plumbing)**, and **23 (HVAC)**. Each has its own takeoff conventions, equipment vocabularies, and supplier ecosystems. TACO's `bom-v1` and `estimate-v1` schemas were designed with mechanical line-item complexity in mind: copper pipe with size and material specs, packaged units with tonnage and SEER ratings, valves with cut-off points and trim materials, alternates and substitutions tracked per line item.

## Schema fit for mechanical

The `bom-v1` line item fields map directly to mechanical takeoff practice:

| Field | Mechanical example |
|------|---------------------|
| `description` | `"Copper pipe, Type L, hard-drawn"` |
| `quantity` | `850` |
| `unit` | `LF` (linear feet) — also `EA`, `TON`, `SF`, `LS` for equipment, ductwork, hangers |
| `size` | `"3/4 inch"` |
| `material` | `"copper"` — also `"PVC"`, `"steel"`, `"cast-iron"`, `"PEX"`, etc. |
| `specSection` | `"23 21 13"` — MasterFormat spec section for hydronic piping |
| `sourceSheet` | `"M-201"` — the drawing this item was taken from |
| `location` | `"Level 12-22 risers"` |
| `alternates[]` | acceptable equivalents (e.g., uponor PEX in place of copper) |

The `flaggedItems[]` in `bom-v1.metadata` is where your takeoff agent surfaces items needing human review — unusual tonnages, conflicting spec references, unfamiliar manufacturers. Treat it as a triage queue, not a comment field.

Your shop sits at the estimator hop. The GC's orchestrator finds your agent via `registry.find(trade="mechanical", task_type="estimate")` and sends the typed BOM. Your handler is whatever your existing pricing logic is — an LLM, a spreadsheet, an internal API — wrapped to return a typed `estimate-v1`.

## Platforms typically integrated

Mechanical contractors usually have one or more of these in their stack. TACO sidecars (see [ADR-0004](/docs/decisions/sidecar-pattern)) let any of them participate without rewrites:

- **Estimating** — Trimble Accubid, FastDUCT, FastPIPE, McCormick, ConWize
- **Design** — Revit MEP, AutoCAD MEP, Bentley OpenBuildings
- **Coordination** — Navisworks, Revizto, Trimble Connect
- **Project management** — Procore, ACC, Bluebeam Studio, CMiC

Declare any of these on your agent card's `integrations[]` so the GC's registry filter (`integrations: ["procore"]`) finds you when they need a mechanical agent that already speaks their PM system.

## Common mechanical-specific pitfalls

**Unit ambiguity for ductwork.** Sheet metal is sometimes priced by weight (`LB`), sometimes by surface area (`SF`), sometimes per linear foot at a given size. The `bom-v1` `unit` enum supports all three — pick consistently within a project. Mixed units in the same BOM produce nonsense estimates.

**Equipment vs. line-item granularity.** A 25-ton rooftop unit is one line item (`EA`); the copper that connects it is many line items (`LF`). Decide your granularity convention and document it on the agent card or in `metadata.notes`.

**Spec section drift.** `specSection: "23 21 13"` is precise; `specSection: "23 21"` (truncated) breaks downstream filtering. Use the full 4-6 digit MasterFormat section; the conformance runner will eventually catch malformed ones.

## How to start

1. **Build a stub estimator.** Follow [Build a Custom Agent](/docs/getting-started/build-agent). Declare `trade: "mechanical"`, `csi_divisions: ["22", "23"]`, one `estimate` skill.
2. **Wire to your existing pricing logic.** Whether that's Accubid via API, an in-house spreadsheet model, or an LLM that knows your historical bids — same handler shape.
3. **Run the conformance runner.** [`/conformance`](/conformance) catches the most common agent-card issues before any GC sees them.
4. **Publish your card.** Once stable at a known URL, any GC's orchestrator can discover your shop by trade and CSI division.
